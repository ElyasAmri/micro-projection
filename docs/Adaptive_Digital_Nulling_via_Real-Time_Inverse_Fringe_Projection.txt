Title: Adaptive Digital Nulling via Real-Time Inverse Fringe Projection for High-Resolution In-Situ Metrology in Additive Manufacturing 

Abstract: High-precision optical metrology for Additive Manufacturing (AM) faces a fundamental trade-off between lateral dynamic range and 
vertical resolution. While laser and white-light interferometry offer sub-nanometer sensitivity, they are critically limited by fringe density saturation
on the high-slope geometries and high-frequency textures typical of laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) parts. Conversely, macro-scale 3D methods such as 
stereovision provide robust tracking but lack the axial resolution required for surface roughness characterization. This paper proposes a real-time, 
adaptive "digital nulling" methodology using a high-speed Digital Light Processing (DLP) projector to overcome these constraints. By projecting 
object-adapted, pre-deformed fringes, the system optically filters low-frequency surface form (waviness) in real time. This "optical flattening" 
enables the projection of high-frequency gratings that would otherwise exceed the Nyquist limit of the imaging sensor, effectively decoupling the measurement 
of micro-scale roughness from macro-scale topography. Unlike traditional null optics, our approach requires no prior CAD data or precise system calibration,
generating inverse patterns dynamically from an initial "golden part" reference. Experimental results on an AM-simulated platform demonstrate a significant 
enhancement in vertical sensitivity and slope-handling capacity compared to classical fringe projection. This flexible, DLP-driven architecture provides a 
high-throughput solution for in-situ quality assurance of complex microstructures and layer-wise surface finish monitoring in advanced manufacturing 
environments.
